=============================================================================
ALGORITHM: DIFFIE-HELLMAN KEY EXCHANGE (1976)
TYPE:      Key Agreement Protocol
STATUS:    The "One-Trick Pony" of Public Key Cryptography
=============================================================================

1. THE CORE CONCEPT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Diffie-Hellman (DH) was the first system to use Public and Private keys, but 
it does not "encrypt" messages and it does not "sign" documents.

Its specific purpose is to allow two parties (Alice and Bob) who have never 
met to agree on a shared password (Shared Secret) over an insecure channel, 
without an eavesdropper (Eve) figuring out what that password is.

Think of the keys as "ingredients" for a recipe, rather than locks and keys.


2. THE MECHANISM (THE "PAINT MIXING" ANALOGY)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Imagine mixing colors rather than math:

[Public] Common Color:  Yellow (Known to everyone, even Eve)

STEP A: Alice & Bob pick "Private Colors" (Private Keys)
- Alice picks RED (Secret)
- Bob picks BLUE (Secret)

STEP B: They mix their secret with the public color (Public Keys)
- Alice: Yellow + Red = ORANGE. (Alice sends ORANGE to Bob)
- Bob:   Yellow + Blue = GREEN. (Bob sends GREEN to Alice)
* Eve sees Yellow, Orange, and Green. She cannot un-mix them to find Red or Blue.

STEP C: They add their secret to what they received (The Shared Secret)
- Alice takes Bob's GREEN and adds her secret RED  = BROWN sludge.
- Bob takes Alice's ORANGE and adds his secret BLUE = BROWN sludge.

RESULT:
Alice and Bob now have the same color (BROWN) to use as a key.
Eve cannot make Brown because she lacks the private Red or Blue ingredients.


3. THE MATHEMATICAL LOGIC
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The math relies on the "Discrete Logarithm Problem."
g = public base (generator)
p = public prime number

1. Alice picks private integer (a). Calculates (g^a mod p) -> Sends to Bob.
2. Bob picks private integer (b). Calculates (g^b mod p) -> Sends to Alice.
3. Alice takes Bob's result and raises it to power of (a).
4. Bob takes Alice's result and raises it to power of (b).

Both result in the same number: (g^ab mod p).


4. THE LIMITATIONS (WHY IT IS NOT RSA)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
While revolutionary, Diffie-Hellman has significant functional gaps:

A. NO DIRECT ENCRYPTION
   You cannot use a DH Public Key to encrypt a message like "Attack at Dawn."
   You can only use it to derive a number to use for a *different* algorithm 
   (like AES) to encrypt the message.

B. NO DIGITAL SIGNATURES (Non-Repudiation)
   Because the math is about mixing values, not reversible trapdoors, 
   Alice cannot "sign" a document. There is no way to prove a message came 
   from Alice using only DH.

C. NO AUTHENTICATION (Man-in-the-Middle Attack)
   This is the critical flaw. DH does not verify *who* is on the other end.
   - Alice shouts her public key to Bob.
   - Eve intercepts it.
   - Eve pretends to be Bob and sends her own public key to Alice.
   - Alice thinks she established a secure line with Bob, but she actually 
     established a secure line with Eve.
