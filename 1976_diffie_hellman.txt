1. THE "MAGIC TRICK" (1976)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before this, you had to meet in a dark alley to swap codes.
Diffie and Hellman figured out how to shout secrets across a crowded room
without anyone understanding them.
- It’s like mixing paint.
- I send you Yellow. You add your secret Red.
- You send me Blue. I add my secret Green.
- We both end up with the exact same muddy Brown color, but the spies listening
  only saw the bright starting colors. They can't "un-mix" the paint.

2. WAS IT CRACKED? (THE MATH VS. THE REALITY)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Technically, NO. The core logic (the "paint mixing") has never been broken.
However, it has two massive holes that spies exploit:

A. The "Small Bowl" Problem (Logjam)
   In the 70s and 90s, computers were weak, so people used small numbers
   (small mixing bowls).
   - Modern supercomputers can now "un-mix" those small batches.
   - The NSA reportedly spent billions building a machine that cracked the
     most common "mixing bowl" (prime number) used by the entire internet,
     allowing them to read VPN traffic for years.
   - Fix: We just use much, much bigger numbers now.

B. The "Blind Guy" Problem (Man-in-the-Middle)
   This is the fatal flaw of the original design.
   - The algorithm connects Alice to Bob, but it doesn't check ID cards.
   - If Eve sits in the middle and pretends to be Bob, Alice agrees on a
     secret code with Eve, thinking it's Bob.
   - Diffie-Hellman creates a secure pipe, but it doesn't tell you who is
     on the other end of it.
