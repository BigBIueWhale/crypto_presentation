1. THE UNSETTLING ORIGIN
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHA-256 was not born in a university classroom. It was designed behind closed
doors by the NSA and published in 2001 as a federal standard.

While the world trusts it to secure trillions of dollars in banking and
crypto, we rely on it based on a terrifying assumption: "Nobody has cracked
it *yet*."

Unlike the One-Time Pad (Encryption), which is mathematically proven to be
unbreakable, there is NO mathematical proof that SHA-256 is secure. It is
entirely possible that a shortcut exists, and perhaps the NSA already knows
it. We are betting the global economy on a math problem we hope is hard.

3. BITCOIN'S PARANOIA (THE DOUBLE TAP)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
When Satoshi Nakamoto designed Bitcoin in 2008, he looked at SHA-256 and
saw a potential weakness. He feared "Length Extension Attacks"—a flaw where
an attacker can append data to a hash without knowing the secret.

Because of this, and general paranoia about the longevity of the algorithm,
Bitcoin almost never uses SHA-256 once. It uses "SHA-256d."

The Formula: Final_Hash = SHA256( SHA256( Data ) )

It performs the calculation, takes the result, and hashes it again. It is a
desperate mathematical shield—a hope that if the algorithm is wounded, the
second layer will prevent the system from bleeding out.
