=============================================================================
ALGORITHM: BASE58CHECK
INVENTOR:  Satoshi Nakamoto
TYPE:      Binary-to-Text Encoding
STATUS:    The "Human" Interface
=============================================================================

1. THE PROBLEM (BASE64 VS HUMANS)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Computers like binary. Humans like text.
The standard way to turn binary into text is Base64 (used in email).
But Base64 includes:
- 0 (Number Zero)
- O (Capital Letter O)
- I (Capital Letter I)
- l (Lowercase Letter L)

In a printed font, these look identical. If you hand-type a Bitcoin address
and mistake an 'l' for an '1', the money is burned forever.

2. THE ALGORITHM (BASE58)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Satoshi took Base64 and physically removed the 6 ambiguous characters.
He also removed symbols like '+' and '/' to ensure the address could be
double-clicked and highlighted as a single word.

3. THE "CHECK" (ERROR CORRECTION)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
If you mistype a single letter in a bank account number, the money goes to
a stranger. In Bitcoin, the address effectively "spell checks" itself.

The Formula:
1. Take the Payload (The RIPEMD hash).
2. Double Hash it: SHA256(SHA256(Payload)).
3. Take the first 4 bytes (Checksum).
4. Glue them to the end of the Payload.

When a wallet reads an address, it runs the math again. If the checksum
doesn't match the payload, the wallet screams "INVALID ADDRESS" and refuses
to send.
