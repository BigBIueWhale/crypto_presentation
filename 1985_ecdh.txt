=============================================================================
ALGORITHM: ECDH (Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman)
INVENTED:  1985 (Neal Koblitz & Victor Miller)
STATUS:    The "Titanium" Upgrade (Lighter, Stronger, Faster)
=============================================================================

1. THE PROBLEM WITH RSA (THE BLOAT)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
By the 2000s, RSA was getting fat.
To keep RSA secure against faster computers, the keys had to get exponentially
larger.
- An RSA key is now like a massive 4-inch thick steel vault door.
- It’s secure, but it’s heavy. It makes your phone battery die and slows down
  web servers.
- We needed something that offered "Vault Door" security with the weight of a
  credit card.

2. THE MECHANISM (GEOMETRY VS. ARITHMETIC)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Is it fundamentally different? YES.
- RSA relies on factoring massive numbers (Arithmetic).
- ECDH relies on points bouncing around a curved line (Algebraic Geometry).

The "Billiards" Analogy:
Imagine a weirdly shaped billiards table (the Elliptic Curve).
1. You place a ball at a specific spot.
2. You hit it so it bounces precisely 5,000,000 times.
3. It lands at a final coordinate.

The Trapdoor:
It is trivial to hit the ball 5 million times and find the end point.
But if you only see the start point and the end point, it is mathematically
impossible to figure out how many times it bounced to get there.

3. WHY IT IS BETTER THAN RSA
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
It comes down to efficiency. ECDH provides the same security with a fraction
of the bits.

The Exchange Rate:
- RSA Key (3072 bits) = Gold bar (Heavy, hard to move).
- ECC Key (256 bits)  = Diamond (Tiny, easy to move, same value).

Because the keys are tiny:
1. Mobile phones can encrypt data without draining the battery.
2. Web connections (SSL/TLS) happen instantly.
3. It fits on tiny chips (Smart Cards, Passports).

* RSA is still used because it's older and simpler to understand, but ECDH
  (and its cousin ECDSA) is the actual engine running the modern mobile web.
