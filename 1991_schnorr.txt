1. THE PATENT TRAGEDY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Schnorr signatures are mathematically simpler and better than ECDSA.
Why didn't Satoshi use them in 2008?
Claus Schnorr held a patent on them until early 2008. They were not "open"
standard. Satoshi had to use the clunkier ECDSA to avoid lawsuits.

2. LINEARITY (THE MATH MAGIC)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
ECDSA is non-linear. You cannot just "add" two signatures together.
Schnorr IS linear.

   Sig(A) + Sig(B) = Sig(A+B)

3. KEY AGGREGATION (THE CLOAK)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
In ECDSA, if 3 people sign a transaction (Multi-Sig), you see 3 signatures
on the blockchain. It is obvious and heavy.

In Schnorr, 3 people can combine their keys into one "Aggregate Key."
They combine their signatures into one "Aggregate Signature."

To the blockchain, it looks like a single person signed it.
- Privacy: No one knows it was a multi-sig.
- Scale: 3 signatures take up the space of 1.

4. BEST PRIVACY YOU CAN GET ON BITCOIN (IN SIMPLE TERMS)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
We only talk about the *best* case:
- You use Taproot addresses (start with "bc1p…").
- You use Schnorr signatures.
- You use multi-party transactions (CoinJoin-style).

Think of the blockchain as a public notebook where every payment is written
down forever.

WHAT THE BLOCKCHAIN STILL SHOWS
- How many coins go in and how many coins go out (all amounts are public).
- Which “old coins” were spent and which “new coins” were created.
  (Everyone can follow the chain of ownership step by step.)
- That you used Taproot (special outputs marked as “bc1p…”).
- One public key for each Taproot coin.
- One Schnorr signature for each coin being spent.

WHAT THE BLOCKCHAIN DOES *NOT* SHOW IN THIS BEST CASE
- Whether a public key comes from:
    - one person, or
    - several people who combined their keys into one “group key”, or
    - a more complex rule behind the scenes.
- Any backup or emergency rules that were never used.
- In a well-designed multi-party transaction with similar output amounts:
    - exactly which input paid which output.

So: the notebook still shows *all* money amounts and *all* links between
coins, but hides some of the internal rules and exact pairings.
